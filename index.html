Mar 2026 — Sept 2026 · Master Thesis · ETH Zürich

Polygraph: Large-Scale Multi-Session Trajectory Optimization Using Pose Graphs

Robots, vehicles and AR devices revisit the same places many times, and every visit yields a trajectory that drifts and lives in its own coordinate frame. Polygraph merges them into one globally consistent set online, one keyframe cluster at a time, without ever building a 3D map.

Loop-closure candidates come from learned image retrieval, are verified with sparse feature matching and monocular metric depth, and only enter the shared pose graph once independent evidence agrees. Evaluated on cars, HoloLens, iPad, Boston Dynamics Spot and handheld routes, it runs faster than the closest prior work while matching or improving its accuracy.

Mar 2025 — Present · ETH Zürich

CroCoDL: Cross-device Collaborative Dataset for Localization

A large-scale benchmark for visual localization across very different devices. Seven new indoor and outdoor locations are captured with a Boston Dynamics Spot, HoloLens 2, iPhones and iPads, NavVis scanners and a DJI drone, with every device aligned to dense reference meshes so trajectories can be co-localized in one frame.

I work on processing and maintaining the data, and co-organised the CroCoDL localization challenge at the ICCV 2025 workshop.

Aug 2025 — Dec 2025 · Semester Project · ETH Zürich

Interactive 3D Segmentation

Training interactive segmentation models needs accurate ground-truth 3D object masks, but annotating them usually means rounds of positive and negative clicks to fix every boundary, which is slow and tedious.

This project removes the back-and-forth: from a single initial click, it produces a high-quality 3D segmentation of the object, building on Segment Anything. Annotation effort drops sharply while segmentation accuracy is preserved.

Oct 2024 — Mar 2025 · ETH Zürich

Spot-On: A Mixed Reality Interface for Multi-Robot Cooperation

A mixed reality framework that lets a person direct multiple Boston Dynamics Spot robots through a HoloLens. You look at a drawer, swing door or light switch, pick an action from a floating menu, and the robots carry it out together in the real room.

In a user study, participants rated the design and usability “good” or “very good” in almost all cases.

Sept 2024 — Mar 2025 · ETH Zürich

HoloSpot: Intuitive Object Manipulation via Mixed Reality Drag-and-Drop

Remote control of robots for places where people shouldn’t go, such as disaster sites or areas with radiation or toxic chemicals. A scanned room appears in the HoloLens as a miniature “dollhouse” hologram: drag an object to a new spot, and a Boston Dynamics Spot picks it up and places it there in the real room.

Built in Unity on top of the Spot-Compose framework, with AnyGrasp for grasping and an interactive tutorial for new users. End-to-end pick-and-place tests and a user study confirmed the controls are intuitive.

Mar 2024 — Aug 2024 · Semester Project · ETH Zürich

Multiple to One Matching

When a scene has already been reconstructed with Structure from Motion, matching a new query image against every database image one pair at a time wastes work. This project matches the query against many images at once.

Keypoints from the database images are grouped by the 3D point they observe and aggregated, then matched in a single pass by a network built on LightGlue. This makes matching faster and more accurate for localization and for extending reconstructions, and the method is integrated into the HLoc pipeline.

Mar 2023 — Jul 2023 · Bachelor Thesis · University of Belgrade

Stereo Vision Depth Estimation

A camera flattens the 3D world into a 2D image and loses depth along the way. This thesis rebuilds stereo depth estimation from first principles: the linear camera model, intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, lens distortion and calibration, then depth from calibrated stereo and from uncalibrated stereo through the fundamental matrix, correspondence search and rectification.

The second half puts the theory to work in a real-time depth pipeline on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano with a Sony IMX219 stereo camera (60 mm baseline). Built with OpenCV, it calibrates and rectifies the cameras, computes and filters disparity maps, and converts them to depth. CUDA acceleration makes live video processing possible on a 5 W embedded board.
